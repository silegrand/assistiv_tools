Cluster 02 — Coordination & Navigation
Conceptual tool — full build reserved for funded development

What comes next, who to contact,
and what exists to help, before hitting rock bottom.

The system does not volunteer information. Carers find grants, assessments, and support groups only by searching independently, often after the moment when they would have been most useful. This tool pushes what is relevant before the carer has to ask.

"She navigated signposting loops for hours, following contact leads only to be redirected endlessly. She found groups, grants, and assessments only by searching independently, often after the moment when they would have been most useful."
Primary source: carer of a stroke and cardiac surgery survivor, sole coordinator across nine NHS departments
The core distinction

A navigation tool is not a signposting directory.

A directory waits to be searched. It places the burden of knowing what to look for on someone who is, by definition, in the worst position to know what exists.

A navigation tool pushes relevant information at the right moment in the journey, before the carer knows they need it. The difference is the difference between active and passive. Between arriving before the crisis and arriving after it.

Once a resource has been surfaced, the decision and the relationship belong to the carer. The tool makes action as easy as possible, a direct link, a template message, a record that the step was taken, but it does not make the decision for them.

What the tool does

Proactive surfacing, then enabling action

Six functions, working together. The tool does not make decisions for the carer, it removes the invisible barrier between the carer and the resources that exist to help them.

🧭
Journey-aware surfacing
Resources, assessments, and entitlements pushed at the right stage, acute phase, marathon, or approaching transition. Not a list. A signal.
🏛️
Carer's assessment prompt
Surfaced proactively when the situation indicates it is relevant, not after the carer has been caring for two years and never knew they were entitled to one.
👐
Local group finder
Condition-matched and location-aware peer support, surfaced at the right stage, before isolation becomes collapse, not after.
📬
Direct action, not a list
A direct contact link where one exists. A template message where it does not. A record that the step was taken and whether it was followed up.
🔁
Next-step chaining
When one step is taken, the tool knows what typically follows, and surfaces it. The carer is never left at a dead end wondering what to do next.
📋
Action log
Every surfaced resource, every step taken, every open item, recorded. Not as an audit trail, but as a map: where the carer has been, and what is still open.

Design constraints

What this tool must know to work

Coordination and Navigation requires something the other clusters do not, situational awareness. The tool must understand enough about where the carer is to surface what is relevant. Three constraints govern how that awareness is built and used.

01

Lightweight situational awareness

Not a lengthy assessment. Enough signal to know whether the carer is in the acute phase, the marathon, or approaching a transition. The burden of establishing that context must be lower than the burden of not having it.

02

Active, not passive

The tool pushes at the right moment. It does not wait. A resource surfaced six months too late is not a resource. It is a reminder of what was missed. Timing is not a secondary concern; it is the primary one.

03

Decision and relationship belong to the carer

Once a resource has been surfaced, the tool's role is to make action as easy as possible, a direct link, a template, a record that the step was taken. It does not make the decision. It does not follow up on the carer's behalf. It enables.

The safety boundary

What this tool will and will not do

Surfacing something useful is only valuable if what is surfaced leads somewhere. A navigation tool that surfaces a resource and leaves the carer at a dead end has added load, not removed it.

These tools will →

→
Surface proactively Push what is relevant at the right stage of the journey, before the carer has to search for it.
→
Enable action directly A contact link, a template message, a form, not a link to a page that links to another page.
→
Record what happened Log every surfaced resource, every step taken, every item still open. The carer always knows where things stand.
→
Chain to the next step When one step is completed, surface what typically follows, so the carer is never left at a dead end.

These tools will not ✕

✕
Make decisions The tool surfaces options. The carer decides. The tool does not prioritise one resource over another based on anything other than relevance and timing.
✕
Act on the carer's behalf The tool makes action easy. It does not take action. The relationship between the carer and any organisation is the carer's to own.
✕
Surface without a route A surfaced resource with no clear next step is not helpful, it is noise. Every item surfaced must have an actionable path attached.
✕
Replace professional navigation Where a carer needs a named professional, a social worker, a discharge coordinator, a carer support worker, the tool routes to them. It does not substitute for them.

"Does surfacing this resource make the carer's next hour easier or harder? If there is no actionable path attached, it makes it harder."

The design test, applied to every item before it enters the surfacing engine
